Optera Climate — Supplier Outreach

Productizing a backend-only supplier survey process into a self-serve tool that scaled with the business

Role

Director of UX

Company

Optera

Team

Designer, PM, 3 engineers

Domain

Climate-tech · B2B Internal Tools · ESG

Key Outcomes

  • Saved 15+ hours of engineering work per month by productizing what had been a backend-only supplier-survey process — freeing the engineering team to focus on the platform's core climate-data work
  • Reduced consultant response time to customer survey requests by 67%, down to 2 days — turning a hidden bottleneck in the onboarding flow into a competitive advantage as Optera scaled
  • Made the strategic call that this scaling problem was worth a roadmap slot — reframing what looked like an internal-process inefficiency into a UX investment with measurable impact on customer experience
  • Anchored the design team's research practice in the engineering workflow itself: 2 weeks embedded with engineers, building shared documentation that crossed functional silos and produced an artifact multiple teams could contribute to

THE BUSINESS STAKES

When Internal Friction Becomes a Customer Problem

Optera helps companies measure and reduce their carbon footprint — partnering with their customers through in-house consultancy services to determine current emissions and chart a path to net-zero goals. The Supplier Outreach product line is how Optera's customers reach out to their own suppliers to gather emissions data. It's foundational to the entire scope-3 emissions story.

By the second year of running the program, the supplier-outreach process had a hidden problem: it didn't scale. New customer onboarding was outpacing the platform's ability to absorb new survey campaigns, and the gap was being filled by engineers running backend-only processes by hand. That meant Optera's scaling speed was capped by engineering bandwidth on an internal tooling task — not by sales, not by climate-data quality, but by a workflow that should have been a product capability.

KEY DECISIONS & TRADEOFFS

Reframing an Internal Process as a Product

The situation arrived as an internal-efficiency complaint — engineering was being pulled away from product work. The instinct in many orgs would be to triage that as a process fix: better hand-offs, clearer documentation, maybe a runbook. The team made a different call.

The work belonged on the product roadmap. The same friction that was costing engineering hours was also costing consultants speed in responding to customers. Productizing the supplier-outreach workflow as a self-serve tool would do two things at once: free engineering capacity and shorten the customer-facing response loop. That reframe — from internal-process fix to customer-experience product investment — is what made the project earn a roadmap slot in the first place.

APPROACH

Two Weeks Embedded with Engineering

Discovery on this project meant working alongside engineering for two weeks before any design work began. The team mapped every backend process and documented the technical reality — what each script did, what data it touched, what manual steps were involved — into artifacts that consultants, engineers, and the design team could all use as a single source of truth. Those documents became the architecture brief for the redesign, and just as importantly, they unblocked the cross-functional conversation that had been stuck in tribal knowledge.

WHAT SHIPPED

A Self-Serve Survey-Management Tool

The shipped tool gave Optera consultants direct control over what had been an engineer-only workflow: creating new supplier-outreach campaigns, managing existing surveys, and updating supplier records without filing a backend ticket. The interaction patterns were designed for the consultants' actual operating cadence — they manage many concurrent campaigns and need to move fast across them — not for the engineers who had been the previous “users.”

Two key flows from the shipped tool, recorded as the team demoed at launch:

Creating a new supplier-outreach campaign — the workflow that used to require filing a backend ticket with engineering.

Editing and managing an active campaign — updating supplier records, adjusting reach, and tracking response status without leaving the consultant's working surface.

Outcomes

15+ engineering hours saved per month · 67% reduction in consultant response time, down to 2 days · engineering capacity returned to platform work · foundation set for further self-serve tooling as Optera continues to scale.

IMPACT & OUTCOMES

A Scaling Bottleneck Turned Into a Customer Advantage

The headline numbers — 15+ hours of engineering recovered each month, customer response cycle cut to two days, a 67% improvement — tell the obvious story. The less-obvious story is the cultural one: a workflow problem that had been quietly shaping how Optera scaled stopped being a constraint and became a capability. That kind of shift is the highest-leverage outcome design can produce inside an operating business.

Downstream, the self-serve workflow reshaped how Optera onboarded new customers. The campaign-creation step that had previously been gated by a consultant call collapsed into something a supplier-success manager could lead in the first onboarding session. That removed a soft bottleneck in the sales-to-activation handoff and shortened the time from contract signed to customer's first usable report — the kind of compounding outcome that's easy to underweight on a roadmap and hard to overstate in practice.

RETROSPECTIVE

What I'd Do Differently

The cross-functional discovery worked, but I'd front-load consultant interviews even earlier. We treated the consultants as the bottleneck to remove rather than the experts whose pattern-knowledge needed to be encoded in the tool, and a few of the early prototypes felt like solutions imposed on them rather than designed with them. Spending another week with two or three of our most experienced consultants before any wireframe came out would have surfaced workflow edge cases we eventually patched in the next release. I'd also stage the consultant-side rollout more deliberately — we launched the supplier-facing tool and the consultant dashboard updates close together, and the consultants would have benefited from a quiet beta period to recalibrate their workflows before customers started arriving with new questions.
